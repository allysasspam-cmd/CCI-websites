YOUTH MENTAL HEALTH

Mental health within Kenyan youth: Why are we still so silent?

When I was younger, I suffered from a severe form of allergic rhinitis. My symptoms made my eyes watery and my nose runny, and the children around me teased me for my noisy breathing and teary eyes. I had few friends and often spent time alone.

At the age of nine, I received surgery that helped me breathe and sleep better. My eyes stopped watering, and the bullying finally stopped. But while the surgery addressed my physical health, it did not address the emotional impact bullying had left on my mental health.

No one talked about mental health. I did not know how to express what I was feeling, whether I would be taken seriously, or what I could do about it.

Mental health is closely connected to wellbeing, relationships and learning, yet young people may still find it difficult to speak openly or seek support. CCI aims to help change this by promoting mental health education, reducing stigma and creating safer, more supportive environments for young people in Kenya.

We envision a future where mental wellbeing is at the forefront of adolescent education, and every young person feels that their experience matters.
